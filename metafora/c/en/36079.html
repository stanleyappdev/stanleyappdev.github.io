Choosing a profession is not a life sentence

  • Vocation
  • Decision-making
  • Uncertainty

The fear of choosing the wrong profession is often more paralyzing than the uncertainty itself. A teen finishing middle or high school, like an adult about to change jobs, feels they are choosing once and for all. That isn’t so, and it helps to say it directly.

What’s worth knowing

- Many people change fields, or their direction within a profession, several times over their lives. A first specialty is a point of entry, not a lifelong label.

- Many skills transfer: the ability to write, negotiate, organize and learn will come in handy anywhere.

- Interest often grows once a person starts to get good at something. Confidence usually comes after a few steps, not before the first one.

- Refusing to choose is also a choice: time passes, but experience doesn’t accumulate.

How to lower the stakes

- Frame the choice as a hypothesis: “I’ll try this field and review the decision in a year.”

- Favor options that keep doors open.

- Decide in advance what signs will tell you it’s time to change course.

Questions: what is the scariest thing about making a mistake? What will you do if, a year from now, you realize this isn’t for you?
