Parable

Look at your fish

  • Boredom
  • Presence
  • Motivation

A young student came to study with a famous naturalist. The naturalist set a tin pan with a fish in front of him, said, “Look at it; later you’ll tell me what you saw,” and left.

In ten minutes the student had seen, it seemed, everything. After an hour he could barely contain his boredom: he paced the room, came back to the fish, walked away again. In desperation he picked up a pencil and began to draw it - and suddenly noticed things he hadn’t seen before. For example, that the fish had no eyelids.

The teacher came back, listened and said, “That’s better. Keep looking.” Three days passed this way. Later the student called them the most valuable lesson of his life.

Questions

  • ✓What seems boring to you because you only look at it for the first minute?
  • ✓What is your “pencil” - a way to look more closely?
  • ✓When has slow, attentive looking already helped you see something new in a person or a situation?

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Based on Samuel Scudder’s essay “In the Laboratory with Agassiz” (1874) about studying with the naturalist Louis Agassiz. Free retelling by Metafora.
