Art technique

A bridge between people

  • Closeness
  • Personal boundaries
  • Loss

Goal: explore the relationship with a particular person through the image of a bridge: what connects you now, what separates you and what kind of connection you want.

Approximate time: 25-35 minutes.

Materials: A4 or A3 paper, pencils, markers or pastels.

  1. 1.Choose the relationship

Ask the client to choose one person whose relationship they want to explore: someone close with whom things are difficult now, or someone with whom contact has been lost. If the topic is painful, for example after a loss or a break-up, agree how deep you will go today.

  1. 2.Draw

“Draw yourself on one side and this person on the other. Between you, draw the bridge as it is now. If there is no bridge, or it is broken, draw that too.”

  1. 3.Explore the bridge

Start with an invitation: “Tell me about this bridge.” Then choose a few questions.

  • ✓What is it made of? What in this relationship matches that material?
  • ✓What is under the bridge? How is that connected with what is happening between you?
  • ✓Who walks across the bridge, and in which direction? Who more often takes a step towards the other?
  • ✓Which part of the bridge is the most reliable, and which the shakiest?
  1. 4.Show the desired bridge

Invite the client to show, in another color or on a separate sheet, what they would like the bridge to be. It doesn’t have to be closer or stronger: sometimes railings, a gate or more distance are needed.

  • ✓How is the desired bridge different from the current one?
  • ✓What of this depends on you, and what on the other person?
  1. 5.Close

Ask the client to name one action or decision about this relationship: write a message, agree on a boundary, leave things as they are for now. If the bridge is connected with a loss, discuss how the bond can be kept in memory, and check how the client feels.

With teenagers

The relationship may be with a friend, parents, a brother or sister, an ex. Talk about who takes steps towards whom and where the teenager wants a boundary.

Note for the practitioner

The material, size and position of the bridge have no ready-made meanings: discuss only what the client links with the image. Don’t suggest building a bridge to someone who has hurt the client; if they choose that relationship themselves, focus on safety and boundaries.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN
