Procrastination: how it works and how to overcome it

  • Motivation
  • Perfectionism
  • Acting despite fear
  • Uncertainty
  • Decision-making
  • Self-compassion
  • Awareness of emotions

Researchers increasingly describe procrastination not only as a time-management problem but as a way of regulating emotions. A person puts off not so much the task as the unpleasant feeling linked to it: fear of failure, boredom, uncertainty, shame about insufficient quality.

The mechanism: putting things off brings instant relief, and the brain remembers this as a working strategy. The guilt afterward intensifies the unpleasant feeling and makes the next attempt even harder.

What does not work: stricter plans, self-criticism, promises to start “on Monday”. In one well-known study, students who forgave themselves for procrastinating on studying for a first exam procrastinated less before the next one.

What works: shrink the step to a size that doesn’t trigger resistance (two minutes), start with the most unpleasant part at your best time of day, remove uncertainty-instead of “prepare the report,” phrase it as “open the file and write the title.”

A question for the client that often gets things moving: what exactly do you feel a second before you reach for your phone?

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Research on procrastination as emotion regulation: Timothy Pychyl and Fuschia Sirois; the role of self-forgiveness was shown in a study by Wohl, Pychyl and Bennett.
