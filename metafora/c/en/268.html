Body-based practice

Dragon breathing

  • Self-regulation
  • Aggression in children
  • Anger
  • Self-soothing
  • Expressing emotions

Goal: use play to show the child a long out-breath that helps them slow down after excitement or lively activity.

Approximate time: 3-5 minutes.

Materials: a paper “flame”: a strip of paper about 3 by 15 cm, cut into tongues along one edge and glued to a pencil.

  1. 1.Show the dragon

“The dragon breathes in through its nose and then breathes out fire through its mouth for a long, long time.” Show it yourself: a calm breath in through the nose and a long breath out through slightly open lips.

  1. 2.Blow on the flame

The child holds the flame 15-20 cm in front of them and breathes out so that the paper tongues move for as long as possible. Suggest trying a “strong” fire and a “quiet long” fire and comparing them.

  1. 3.Limit the number

Two or three dragon breaths are enough, followed by a few ordinary breaths. If the child says they feel dizzy, stop and breathe normally.

  1. 4.Ask
  • ✓What kind of fire did you make: strong or long?
  • ✓What do you notice in your body after the breaths?
  • ✓When might your dragon need a breath like this?

In a group

The children sit in a circle, each with their own flame, and breathe out together at the leader’s signal, not toward a neighbor. There’s no need for a “who’s strongest” contest: it’s more interesting to see whose flame flutters longer.

Note for the practitioner

A long out-breath doesn’t necessarily calm a child right away. Ask what has changed and accept “nothing” as an answer.
