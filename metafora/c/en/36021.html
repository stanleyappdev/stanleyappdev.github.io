Exercise

Staying with the good

  • Joy
  • Presence
  • Resources

Pleasant moments usually flash by in a second: the brain remembers threats much faster than pleasant things. The exercise teaches you to linger on a small joy so that it has time to “sink in.”

How to run

  1. 1.Ask the client to recall something pleasant and ordinary from the past 24 hours: a warm cup in their hands, a message from a friend, sunshine on the windowsill.
  1. 2.Have them describe this moment in detail: what they saw, what they heard, what it smelled like, what the temperature was.
  1. 3.Invite them to stay with this memory for 15–20 seconds and notice how the body responds to it.
  1. 4.Ask: “What was most precious to you in that moment?”
  1. 5.Homework: twice a day, notice one such moment and linger on it for a few breaths longer than usual.

What to pay attention to

Joy is sometimes accompanied by anxiety (“something bad is about to happen”) or guilt. Name these feelings and, as far as possible, return to the pleasant. If that doesn’t work, this too is material for conversation.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Based on Rick Hanson’s idea of “taking in the good” (Hardwiring Happiness, 2013).
