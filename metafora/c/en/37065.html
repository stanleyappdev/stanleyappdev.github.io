Exercise

Three sources of meaning

  • Meaning in life
  • Values
  • Resources

A worksheet for a person who feels there is nothing important in their life, or who clings to a single source of meaning (work, a child) and is afraid of losing it.

How to run

  1. 1.Explain that Frankl distinguished three groups of values: creative (work, helping others), experiential (love, beauty, nature) and attitudinal - the stance we take toward what cannot be changed.
  1. 2.Divide the sheet into three columns:
  • ✓“What I create and do”
  • ✓“What I experience and love”
  • ✓“How I hold on where I can’t change anything.”

In each column the client writes three to five examples from the past year, specifically:

  • ▫not “nature” but “morning walks with the dog by the pond.”
  1. 3.The third column is the hardest. Help with a question: “What was difficult and beyond your control? What helped you stay yourself?” Quiet things count too: I didn’t become bitter, I kept my sense of humor, I let myself cry.
  1. 4.Mark the examples that still give you strength now.

Questions

  • ✓Which column is almost empty? What could you add to it this week?
  • ✓What will support you if your main source is closed off for a while?

In a group, everyone shares only what they want to.

Caution: if the third column touches on a fresh loss or acute pain, put it aside: first acknowledge the pain.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Based on Viktor Frankl’s teaching on the three paths to meaning.
