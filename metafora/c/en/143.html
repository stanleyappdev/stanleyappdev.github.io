Experiment

Testing a prediction

  • Fear
  • Anxiety
  • Uncertainty

KEY IDEA

thoughts are not facts

Goal: learn to write down a prediction before an event and compare it with what happened, to notice how accurate anxious expectations are.

Approximate time: 5-10 minutes before the event, 5-10 minutes after; discussion in the session.

Materials: a sheet with the table below, or notes on a phone.

  1. 1.Before the event

Describe the situation, write down the prediction word for word and rate its probability from 0 to 100%.

  1. 2.After the event

As soon as possible, write down what actually happened: only what could be seen or heard, without new guesses such as “everyone thought…”.

  1. 3.Compare

Did the prediction come true fully, partly or not at all? How likely does it seem now? What did you learn from this?

FieldExample answer
SituationI’ll ask a clarifying question in class.
My predictionEveryone will laugh.
Probability before the event, %70%
What actually happenedThe teacher explained again; I didn’t hear any laughter.
Probability now, %30%
What I learnedPeople ask clarifying questions in class more often than I thought.

A sample entry. Replace it with your own answers.

Questions

  • ✓Which predictions come true most often, and which don’t?
  • ✓What did you do to keep the prediction from coming true (for example, speaking very quietly or avoiding eye contact)?
  • ✓How does the anxiety before similar situations change after several entries?

Note for the practitioner

Ask for several entries rather than one: conclusions emerge from a series of situations. If a prediction came true, discuss how the client coped and what could be changed next time.
