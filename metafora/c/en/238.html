Visualization

Protector

  • Safety
  • Resources
  • Self-soothing
  • Personal boundaries
  • PTSD

Goal: create the image of a protective figure and experience in imagination being protected in a situation where the person lacked protection at the time.

Approximate time: 25-35 minutes.

Preparation: the client already has skills for returning to the present (support, a safe place, naming objects around). Agree on a stop signal.

  1. 1.Create the figure

“Imagine someone who can protect you: a person, an animal, a hero, an imaginary being. It needs to be strong enough to deal with any threat; realism doesn’t matter here.” Ask about its size, voice, where it stands.

  1. 2.Check the figure here

“Imagine it beside you in this room. What does your body feel?” If the figure brings fear or distrust, change it or choose another.

  1. 3.Choose the situation

For a first time, an episode of moderate weight: humiliation by a teacher, an unfair punishment, not violence. The client describes it briefly, without going into details.

  1. 4.Bring in the protector

“You are there, and at the moment it gets hard, your protector appears. What does it do? What does it say?” The protector stops the person causing harm, stands in between, speaks on the client’s behalf. “What do you need at that moment? The protector can give you that too.”

  1. 5.End the scene in safety

“Where does the protector take you? Stay there until you feel calmer.” Rate the state from 0 to 10.

  1. 6.Come back

Feel the feet, look around the room, name three objects.

  1. 7.Discuss
  • ✓What changed when protection appeared?
  • ✓What was most important in the protector’s actions or words?
  • ✓What did you lack then, and do you lack it now?
  • ✓How could you take care of this in your present life?

Note for the practitioner

Working with real situations where protection was missing is close to imagery rescripting. Working with traumatic memories, violence and PTSD symptoms requires training in trauma-focused therapy. If strong fear, freezing or a sense of unreality appears, stop and return to the present; next time limit the work to steps 1-2.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

The image of a protective figure intervening in a painful scene is used in imagery rescripting in schema therapy: Arnoud Arntz, Anoek Weertman (1999). Treatment of childhood memories: theory and practice. Behaviour Research and Therapy, 37. The instructions are editorial.
