Exercise

Hypothesis testing: an anxious thought

  • Children's fears
  • Anxiety
  • Awareness of thoughts
  • Supportive self-talk
  • Anxious thoughts
  • Decision-making

KEY IDEA

thoughts are not facts

Helps test an anxious thought against the available facts and arrive at a balanced conclusion.

How to run

  1. 1.Choose one situation and write down the child’s specific thought.
  1. 2.Fill in the form, separating observations from assumptions. If information is missing, note it.

A sample entry. Replace it with your own answers.

FieldSample answer
SituationA friend hasn’t replied to my message for an hour.
My thoughtHe doesn’t want to be friends with me anymore.
Facts that support the thoughtThere’s no reply yet. That alone doesn’t explain why.
Facts that don’t fit itYesterday he suggested we play together; I don’t know whether he has seen the message.
A more balanced conclusionI don’t know the reason yet. I can check when I get the chance.

Discussion questions

  • ✓What here is a fact, and what is a prediction?
  • ✓What other explanations fit the facts?
  • ✓What is still missing to draw a conclusion?

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Based on cognitive restructuring in CBT; NHS, “Reframing unhelpful thoughts.”
