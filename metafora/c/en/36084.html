The neutral zone

  • Crisis
  • Adapting to change
  • Uncertainty

William Bridges distinguished between change and transition. Change is an external event: being laid off, moving, divorce, retirement, the children leaving home. Transition is the inner process of adjusting to the new reality. It takes longer and has three phases.

  1. 1.Ending. Something ends, even if the change was wanted, and with it go habits, status, and part of how you saw yourself. This is the time to acknowledge the loss rather than convince yourself that “it’s all for the best.”
  1. 2.The neutral zone. The old no longer works, and the new hasn’t taken shape yet. This is where “I don’t know who I am now” comes up. It can feel confusing, empty, anxious - and at the same time, this is often when new ideas and possibilities appear.
  1. 3.New beginning. New habits, roles, and meaning gradually emerge. Often it is noticed only in hindsight.

What helps in the neutral zone

- don’t demand quick clarity or big decisions of yourself;

- hold on to small rituals and routines;

- try new things in small doses;

- write down your thoughts and observations.

Questions: What has ended for you, and have you acknowledged that loss? What in your life right now is no longer the old but not yet the new?

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Based on William Bridges’s transition model, from his book Transitions: Making Sense of Life’s Changes (1980).
