Book

The Happiness Trap

  • Tolerating difficult emotions

KEY IDEA

control has a cost

Russ Harris, a physician and trainer in Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), explains why trying to feel good all the time and get rid of unpleasant thoughts and feelings often increases suffering. The book introduces the core ACT processes: stepping back from thoughts (defusion), acceptance, contact with the present moment, values and action guided by them. Most chapters include simple exercises for practicing on your own.

Suitable to recommend to clients, not only to practitioners: adults who spend a lot of energy fighting anxiety, sadness or self-criticism. Suggest reading one chapter between sessions and trying one exercise from it, then discuss in session how it went.

Questions

  • ✓Which thought did you recognize as a familiar “trap”?
  • ✓What changed when you tried the exercise?
  • ✓What matters to you enough to act on it even when it’s unpleasant?

Caution: the book does not replace treatment for severe depression or suicidal thoughts.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Russ Harris, The Happiness Trap (2007).
