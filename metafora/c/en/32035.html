Game

Nose, mouth… listen to the word

  • Self-regulation
  • Coordination
  • Active listening
  • Attention and memory

Goal: practice attention, listening to instructions and holding back a habitual movement: doing what you hear, not what you see.

Approximate time: 10-15 minutes.

Participants: 5-20 in a semicircle facing the leader.

Materials: none.

  1. 1.Explain the rule

The leader says “Nose, nose, nose…” and points to their own nose. The children do the same. Then the leader changes the word, for example says “mouth” but points to an ear. The task: point to the part of the face that was named, not the one that was shown. You can point next to your face without touching it.

  1. 2.Warm up slowly

Start with two or three names and a slow pace. If needed, remind them: “Listen to the word.” Then gradually speed up.

  1. 3.Pass on the leader’s role

Once the rule is clear, volunteers take turns leading the game for 1-2 minutes.

“Let’s go” version

Lightly pat your knees and chant: “Let’s go, let’s go…”. The leader breaks the rhythm, names a body part and points to it, or deliberately to another one: says “ear” but points to their tummy. Participants point on themselves to exactly what was named, and the rhythm resumes. If the leader says “your neighbor’s ear”, just point in that direction in the air without touching the other person.

“Nose, floor, ceiling” version

The leader names one of the three words and points, sometimes deliberately in another direction. Participants point to their nose, the floor or the ceiling according to the word.

Discussion

  • ✓What helped more: watching, listening or a slower pace?
  • ✓When was it hardest not to copy the leader’s movement?
  • ✓Where in life do we need to act on words rather than on an example?

Closing

Play the last round very slowly, then everyone points to the “ceiling” together and stretches.

If someone mocks

Mistakes are expected here: nobody is out, and the leader does not comment on who made a mistake. If someone laughs at a participant, stop the round and remind everyone: “Everyone makes mistakes, that’s what makes the game fun.”

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

A widely played group game; no author identified. The description of the rules, the discussion questions and the closing are editorial.
