Parable

The seed and the gardener

  • Control
  • Motivation
  • Uncertainty
  • Circle of influence

KEY IDEA

control has a cost

A boy planted a seed in his little garden bed for the first time. The next morning he ran to look, but the soil was flat. In the evening he dug into it with a finger: the seed lay where it was. He covered it up and left.

This went on for several days. Every morning and every evening the boy dug up the soil to make sure something was happening. The seed sent out a thin white root, but each time he took it out, the root got damaged.

Finally the boy burst into tears and told the old neighbor that nothing was growing. The neighbor looked at the dug-up bed and said:

- It is growing, only under the ground where you can’t see it. Water it and leave it alone. Just check whether the soil is damp.

The boy listened. A week later two tiny leaves came up out of the ground.

Questions

  • ✓Why was it so hard for the boy to wait?
  • ✓What in your life do you “dig up” to check whether it is growing?
  • ✓What could “water it and wait” look like for you?
  • ✓By what signs can you tell the process is going on without disturbing it?

Note for the practitioner

The parable suits work with anxious control and impatience with one’s own progress, including in therapy. Don’t turn it into advice to do nothing: watering and care remain. What matters is telling care apart from constant checking.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

An editorial parable by Metafora, based on common stories about an impatient gardener; no author has been identified.
