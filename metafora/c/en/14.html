Metaphor

A backpack full of stones

  • Sadness
  • Responsibility
  • Helplessness
  • Awareness of emotions
  • Shame and guilt
  • Recognizing and naming emotions
  • Resentment

The image: for years a person has been carrying a backpack full of stones and no longer remembers when they put each stone in. One stone is an old resentment, another is other people’s expectations, a third is guilt for something that was not up to them. The backpack grows heavier, and the back gets used to it and stops noticing the weight.

How to use it

Invite the client to imagine their backpack, or to draw it and label a few stones. First name the stones, and only then decide what to do with them. Some of the weight the person really does carry because it is their responsibility; some can be handed over, put aside or carried differently.

Questions

  • ✓Which stone in your backpack is the heaviest?
  • ✓Which did you put in yourself, and which did others give you to carry?
  • ✓How do you notice the weight of the backpack in your body and your mood?
  • ✓What would change if you set one stone down by the roadside or asked someone to help carry it?

Note for the practitioner

Don’t push the client to “drop everything”: some stones are linked to real duties or losses. What matters is telling apart what the person chooses to carry and what they carry out of habit.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

An editorial metaphor by Metafora; the image of a backpack of stones is common in psychological practice and has no identified author.
