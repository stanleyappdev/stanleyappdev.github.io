Game

Guess whose voice

  • Active listening
  • Friendship
  • Team building
  • Attention and memory

Goal: practice listening attention, remember each other in a new group and add a calm moment to the session.

Approximate time: 10-15 minutes.

Participants: 6-15 children who already know each other a little.

Materials: none.

  1. 1.Explain the rules

A willing guesser sits with their back to the group or in the center of the circle and, if they like, closes their eyes. The leader silently points to one participant. That child says a short phrase in a normal voice, for example “Hi, I’m here.” The guesser names who it was, then turns around and checks.

  1. 2.Play a few rounds

The phrase may be repeated if it was hard to hear. There is no need to disguise the voice. After two or three tries the guesser chooses the next volunteer. No one has to be the guesser if they don’t want to.

  1. 3.Variation with a circle and a rhyme

The guesser stands in the center. The others walk slowly in a circle or stay in their places and say together: “We have gathered in a ring, we look round at everything. Someone says ‘Hop, hop, hop!’ Guess whose voice it is.” The words “hop, hop, hop” are said only by the child the leader silently chose.

Discussion

  • ✓What helped you recognize the voice?
  • ✓Whose voice was easiest to recognize, and why?
  • ✓What was it like to sit with your eyes closed?

Closing

At the end everyone says “Hop, hop, hop!” quietly together, and the leader thanks each child for playing.

If someone mocks

Stop laughter at a wrong guess or imitation of someone’s voice at once: “It’s fine to get it wrong here, our voices are alike.” If a child feels embarrassed, offer them the next role within the group rather than in the center.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

A widely played group game; no author identified. The description of the rules, the discussion questions and the closing are editorial.
