Experiment

The white bear

  • Control
  • Awareness of thoughts
  • Anxious thoughts

KEY IDEA

control has a cost

Goal: test through one’s own experience what happens when you try not to think about something, and discuss how this relates to thoughts the client usually pushes away.

Approximate time: 10 minutes.

Materials: a timer; paper and a pen for making marks.

  1. 1.Explain

“Let’s do a short experiment in two parts of two minutes each. Every time a white bear appears in your head, make a mark on the paper.”

  1. 2.First part

“For two minutes, try not to think about a white bear at all.”

  1. 3.Second part

“Now for two minutes think about whatever you like. The bear may appear or not, however it goes.”

  1. 4.Compare

Count the marks in both parts and talk about the impressions.

  • ✓In which part did the image come back more often?
  • ✓What took more effort?
  • ✓What did you do to avoid thinking about the bear, and did it help?
  • ✓Which thought in your life do you try to push away? What happens to it?
  1. 5.Close

“If attempts to chase a thought away often bring it back, what could you do differently?” For example, notice the thought, name it and return to what you were doing (see the cards “Leaves on a Stream” and “Clouds in the sky”).

With teenagers aged 15-18

The bear can be replaced by a funny image of the teenager’s choice. For discussion: what is hardest not to think about before a test or after an argument?

Note for the practitioner

Results vary: for some people the bear appears less often in the first part. Discuss what actually happened for this client rather than proving a predetermined conclusion. Do not run the experiment with intrusive or traumatic thoughts instead of the bear: that is separate work within a therapy plan.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Daniel M. Wegner, David J. Schneider, Samuel R. Carter III, Teri L. White (1987). Paradoxical Effects of Thought Suppression.
