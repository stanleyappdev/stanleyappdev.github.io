Game

Chain retelling

  • Active listening
  • Communication
  • Team building
  • Attention and memory

Goal: experience first-hand how information changes as it is passed by word of mouth, and find ways to pass on and check what you hear more accurately.

Approximate time: 20-30 minutes.

Participants: 8 or more; 4-6 retellers, the rest are observers.

Preparation: two short made-up stories of 6-8 sentences with specific details: names, numbers, colors, time and place.

  1. 1.Explain the rules and assign roles

4-6 volunteers step outside the door. The rest become observers: each one follows one or two details and silently notes what happens to them. During the round no one prompts, comments or laughs at a retelling.

  1. 2.Read the story

The leader reads the story once to those who stayed. One of the observers becomes the first narrator.

  1. 3.Pass the story along the chain

The first volunteer comes in and the narrator retells what they heard. Then the next one comes in, and now the volunteer retells it to them. And so on to the last participant. In the first round no one may ask questions.

  1. 4.Compare the versions

The last participant retells the story to the whole group, then the leader reads the original. The observers name which details disappeared, changed or appeared.

  1. 5.Second round

A new story and new retellers. Now each listener may ask one question and briefly repeat what they understood. Compare the results of the two rounds.

Discussion

  • ✓Which details disappeared first, and which lasted longer?
  • ✓What changed when you were allowed to ask?
  • ✓Why do people involuntarily fill in what is missing?
  • ✓Where in your life do such chains happen: group chats, rumors, news?

Closing

Each person names one way that will help them pass on or check information more accurately.

If someone mocks

Use only made-up stories, no gossip about real people. Stop comments like “you got it all mixed up” at once: “We are looking at how the story changes, not at who made a mistake.” A participant who felt uncomfortable can be an observer in the next round.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

A widely played group game; no author identified. The description of the rules, the discussion questions and the closing are editorial.
