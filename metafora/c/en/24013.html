Exercise

A meaningful moment

  • Values
  • Resources
  • Closeness
  • Meaning in life

Goal: through a memory of a moment of engagement with life, find a personal value and turn it into a concrete action.

Approximate time: 15-25 minutes.

Materials: a sheet of paper and a pen, if wished.

  1. 1.Invite a memory

“Recall a small moment when you felt truly engaged in life: a conversation, a piece of work, caring for someone, a walk. It doesn’t have to be something big or extraordinarily joyful.”

  1. 2.Unfold the moment

Ask the client to describe it in the present tense and in detail: where you are, who is nearby, what you are doing, what you see and hear. Keep the pace slow; the client may close their eyes if that is more comfortable.

  1. 3.Explore the memory
  • ✓What was most important to you in this moment?
  • ✓What were you doing then, not only feeling?
  • ✓What were you like in that moment?
  • ✓What do you feel now as you remember it?
  1. 4.Name the value

Help the client put into words what shows through the memory: “being present with the people I love,” “making things with my own hands,” “being useful.” Check whether the wording resonates with the client.

  1. 5.Bring it into the week

Choose one action that will express this value this week. Discuss that the same pleasant state may not come back: the action matters in itself.

Note for the practitioner

The memory may bring sadness about what has passed. Acknowledge that feeling: it also shows what the person values.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

The ACT exercise “The Sweet Spot”; Kelly G. Wilson with Troy DuFrene (2009). Mindfulness for Two, pp. 203–209. The session structure, examples and questions were added editorially.
