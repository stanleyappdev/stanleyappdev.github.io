Body-based practice

A confident posture

  • Self-confidence
  • Body awareness
  • Inner support
  • Self-presentation

An experiment for situations that call for confidence: a conversation with a boss, answering at the board in class, an exam, an unpleasant phone call. The client looks for a body position in which the sense of “I can stand my ground” appears. There is no recipe for the “right posture”: different things help different people.

How to run

  1. 1.Ask the client to recall the situation, rate their confidence from 0 to 10, and sit or stand the way they usually do in it.
  1. 2.Change one element at a time, 30 seconds each, rating every time:

- back: slumped → upright;

- shoulders: raised or rounded forward → lowered and open;

- head and gaze: lowered → straight ahead;

- hands: hidden, crossed, fiddling with something → open, resting on the table or thighs;

- legs: crossed, tucked under, or on tiptoe → both feet flat on the floor;

- breathing: held → a slow exhale.

  1. 3.Combine the two or three changes that helped most. To tell confidence from bravado, deliberately exaggerate (chest puffed out, chin up) and compare.
  1. 4.Have the client say a key phrase first in the usual posture, then in the new one: “I have a question,” “I don’t agree with this.” Compare the voice and the feeling.
  1. 5.Find an inconspicuous version that can be used while sitting among other people.

Questions

  • ✓Which change mattered most?
  • ✓What was your usual posture protecting you from?

Do not dismiss the usual posture: it did not come about by chance. With pain or limited mobility, change only what is comfortable.
