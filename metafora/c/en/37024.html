Exercise

Opposite action

  • Fear
  • Shame and guilt
  • Anger
  • Sadness

Goal: learn to notice which action an emotion pushes towards and, in situations where it doesn’t fit the facts or causes harm, deliberately act in the opposite way.

Approximate time: 25-35 minutes in session, then practice during the week.

Materials: paper and a pen.

Every emotion pushes towards an action: fear towards running away, anger towards attacking, shame towards hiding, sadness towards freezing and withdrawing. When an emotion doesn’t fit the facts, or giving in to it means harming yourself, you can deliberately do the opposite.

  1. 1.Choose a situation

The client names a recurring situation in which an emotion gets in the way: avoiding a phone call, staying silent at a meeting, snapping at loved ones. Name the emotion and rate its intensity from 0 to 10.

  1. 2.Check the facts

Does the emotion, or its intensity, fit the situation? Will the action it pushes towards help the client achieve what matters to them? If the emotion fits a real danger, opposite action is not appropriate: move on to problem solving.

  1. 3.Name the urge and the opposite action

Write down what you feel like doing and what the opposite would be. For orientation:

  • ✓Fear of calling the tax office: call today, sitting upright, in a calm voice.
  • ✓Shame at not knowing the answer: ask a question at the meeting, looking people in the eye.
  • ✓Anger at a partner who forgot an agreement: step away for a few minutes, unclench your jaw, try to understand them and say something gentle.
  • ✓Sadness that makes you want to stay in bed all weekend: get up, go for a short walk, message an acquaintance.
  1. 4.Rehearse it fully

The opposite action is carried out fully: with the body, voice, words and thoughts. A polite phrase through clenched teeth won’t work. Ask the client to show the posture, say the first words out loud, imagine doing it, and rate the emotion again.

  1. 5.Discuss
  • ✓What was hardest: starting, keeping the posture or voice, not slipping back into the usual reaction?
  • ✓How did the intensity of the emotion change after the rehearsal?
  • ✓Which thoughts pulled you back towards the usual action?
  1. 6.Close

The client chooses one situation in the coming week and writes down exactly what they will do. The emotion usually weakens not the first time but after several repetitions. At the next meeting, compare its intensity before and after.

Note for the practitioner

With strong fears and traumatic experience, work gradually, starting with situations of moderate intensity.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

The Opposite Action skill from the Emotion Regulation module; Marsha Linehan, DBT Skills Training Manual (2015).
