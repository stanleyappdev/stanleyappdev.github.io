Exercise

One draws, the other guides

  • Communication
  • Active listening
  • Team building
  • Attention and memory

In a pair, one person holds a pencil over a sheet of paper, while the other thinks of a simple line or shape and gives directions in words: “up,” “down,” “left,” “right,” “stop.” Agree on where the top of the sheet is and what counts as a short and a long movement. The person drawing may ask for clarification.

When finished, look at the result and compare it with the original idea. Swap roles. Discuss which words were clear and where more precision was needed.

“House by Instructions” variation

Sit at a table in pairs and stand a folder between you so you can’t see each other’s work. Each person draws a simple house, then hides the drawing. One person takes a new sheet and draws following the partner’s words: “Put the pencil near the top edge,” “Draw a short line down,” “Stop.” The person giving instructions doesn’t see the drawing while the partner is drawing, and doesn’t take the partner’s hand or guide their pencil.

Keep your eyes open: the task is to reproduce an unseen original from words. Compare the drawings, swap roles, then discuss what was harder - explaining precisely or following directions. With consent, show both versions to the group.
