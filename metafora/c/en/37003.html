Wise mind

  • Decision-making
  • Presence
  • Awareness of emotions

A skill for moments when a person is torn between “I want it right now” and “I have to, because it’s the right thing.” DBT distinguishes three states of mind: emotion mind (mood decides everything), reasonable mind (logic only), and wise mind, which integrates both and brings a quiet sense of “yes, this is right.”

How to run

  1. 1.Draw two partly overlapping circles: emotion mind and reasonable mind, with wise mind in the overlap. The client recalls an example of each state.
  1. 2.A 2–3-minute practice: imagine a flat pebble gently swaying as it sinks to the bottom of a clear lake, and follow it, bringing your attention down to the center of your body. Or, on the in-breath, silently say “wise,” and on the out-breath, “mind.”
  1. 3.Once attention has settled, the client asks themselves a question (“Should I take this job?”) and listens for what resonates. If no answer comes, repeat the practice later.

Checking a decision

- Am I taking into account both the facts and my feelings?

- Am I choosing this just to get rid of the tension faster?

A strong emotion can also feel like certainty, but wise mind is calm, not haste. At the peak of an emotion, it’s better to postpone an important decision.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

States of mind and wise mind practices from the mindfulness module; Marsha M. Linehan, DBT Skills Training Manual (2015).
