Worry tree

  • Anxious thoughts
  • Circle of influence
  • Control

A worksheet for people who spend hours running through “what ifs.” It helps tell a worry you can do something about from one that only wears you out.

Take one worry and answer the questions in order.

  1. 1.What exactly am I worried about? Put it in one sentence.
  1. 2.Is this a problem that exists now, or a hypothetical “what if”?
  1. 3.Can I do anything about it?

If yes:

- what exactly can I do?

- when: now, today, on which day?

- write down the first step and the time, do it, and then consciously shift your attention.

If no, or it is just a “what if”:

- tell yourself: “This is a worry I can’t do anything about right now”;

- don’t look for answers online or ask others in order to calm down;

- return to what you were doing or to an activity that needs your full attention: a conversation, movement, working with your hands.

Questions for discussion

- Which worries do you have more of: practical or hypothetical?

- What most often stops you from letting go of a worry you can’t do anything about?

It helps to draw the tree on a sheet of paper: the trunk is the worry, and the two branches are “I can act” and “I can’t influence it.”

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Based on the “worry tree” by Gillian Butler and Tony Hope from the book Manage Your Mind (1995).
