Metaphor

Tug of war with a monster

  • Control
  • Awareness of thoughts
  • Tolerating difficult emotions
  • Shame and guilt
  • Personal boundaries

KEY IDEA

control has a cost

Imagine you are pulling on a rope. At the other end is a monster: anxiety, shame, a craving for something you want to give up. Between you is a chasm. The monster is big and never seems to tire. The harder you pull, the harder it pulls back, and the contest takes all your strength. You cannot win. But there is another option: drop the rope. The monster will not go anywhere; it may growl and pull faces on the other side, but your hands will be free.

The tug-of-war stands for attempts to argue with, defeat or drown out an experience. Dropping the rope does not mean giving up or agreeing with the monster. It means stepping out of a contest that has no winner and getting your hands back for your own life.

When to use it: the client describes an exhausting inner struggle in which the more effort they make, the stronger the resistance.

How to run

The metaphor can be felt physically. Give the client one end of a scarf or towel and pull on the other. Ask them to notice the tension and then simply let go of their end. Talk about what changed in their body and attention.

Questions

  • ✓Who is your monster? What does it say or do?
  • ✓What does your tug-of-war look like: what do you do to win?
  • ✓What happens when you let go of the rope, even for a moment?
  • ✓What would you do with your hands if they were free?

Note for the practitioner

In the physical version, pull gently and agree in advance that the client can let go at any time.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

An ACT metaphor described by Steven C. Hayes.
