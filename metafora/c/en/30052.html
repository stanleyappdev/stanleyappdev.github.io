Ikigai: four circles and your own sources of meaning

  • Vocation
  • Meaning in life
  • Values
  • Your own wants
  • Life path

Helps explore interests, skills, usefulness to others, and opportunities for reward. The four circles are a popular reflection framework that does not capture the full meaning of the Japanese concept of ikigai.

How to run

  1. 1.Fill in the four fields with your own examples.

A sample entry for illustration. Replace it with your own answers.

FieldSample answer
I love: what sparks curiosity or enjoyment?Drawing and explaining complex things with simple diagrams.
I’m good at: what skills does my experience confirm?I made clear diagrams for my study group.
People need: who benefits, and how?Classmates said the diagrams helped them find their way around the topic.
I can be paid for: what is in demand?I assume people pay for designing learning materials; the demand still needs to be checked.
  1. 2.Transfer the answers to four overlapping circles. Mark possible combinations where they overlap.
  1. 3.Separate experience from assumptions. Choose one combination you want to test with a small trial. A single activity in the center is not a required outcome.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

The four-circle chart is Andrés Zuzunaga’s diagram as adapted by Marc Winn (2014).
