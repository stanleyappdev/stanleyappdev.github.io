Film

A Man Called Ove

  • Loss
  • Loneliness
  • Sadness
  • Anger
  • Support

Ove (Rolf Lassgård) is a gloomy Swede approaching sixty who walks round his neighbourhood every morning checking that everyone follows the rules: where they park, where they throw rubbish, whether the gates are shut. A few months ago his wife Sonja died of cancer, and now, after decades of work, he is being pushed into retirement. Ove decides to end his life, but each time something interrupts his plan: new neighbours, the Iranian woman Parvaneh with her husband and daughters, who ask for help from the very first day, a stray cat, someone else’s trouble. Flashbacks gradually reveal his story: his father, who taught him to work with his hands and died on the railway, meeting Sonja on a train, her faith in him, and the accident that left her in a wheelchair and cost them the child they were expecting.

Parvaneh isn’t afraid of Ove: she asks him to teach her to drive, argues with him and invites him to dinner. Ove reluctantly returns to people: he takes in the cat, gives shelter to a young man thrown out of home by his father, and together with the neighbours defends his seriously ill old friend Rune, with whom he once fell out, when social services want to take Rune into a care facility.

Spoiler: Ove eventually abandons his plan and goes on living among his neighbours. Some time later he dies in his sleep because of a weak heart, and many people with whom he had grown close again come to his funeral.

Psychological thread: grief hidden behind anger, rules and control, and a return to connection through small everyday tasks. Themes: loss of a partner, loneliness in old age, retirement, community as support.

Who to suggest it to: adults after the loss of a partner, people who are struggling after retirement; relatives of gloomy, “awkward” older people; for a conversation about how anger can hide pain.

Caution: several suicide attempts (partly presented with humour), an accident and the loss of a pregnancy. Don’t suggest the film to a client with current suicidal thoughts; if they have already seen it, talk about safety directly.

Questions

  • ✓How does Ove treat his neighbours at the beginning of the film? What do you think lies behind his harshness?
  • ✓What did Sonja give Ove? How does her absence show in his days?
  • ✓Why is it Parvaneh who manages to get through to him? What does she do differently from others?
  • ✓What role do small things play in Ove’s return to life: the cat, the driving lessons, helping Rune?
  • ✓Who in your life “knocks on the door” even when you don’t open it?
