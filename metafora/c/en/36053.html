How a panic attack spirals

  • Panic attacks
  • Anxious thoughts
  • Body awareness

Panic stops seeming like chaos once it has a logic to it. It is worth giving the client this explanation as soon as they first describe an attack.

The panic cycle

  1. 1.Trigger: a bodily sensation. A racing heart after climbing stairs, dizziness from the heat, coffee, fatigue.
  1. 2.Interpretation: “Something’s wrong with my heart,” “I’m going to faint,” “I’m going crazy.”
  1. 3.Fear: the body reacts to the thought as a threat, and adrenaline is released.
  1. 4.More sensations: the heart beats harder, breathing speeds up, hands tremble, everything feels unreal.
  1. 5.The new sensations seem to confirm the interpretation, and the cycle spirals.

The peak usually comes within a few minutes, and then the state subsides on its own: the body cannot sustain that level of activation for long. But people often credit their rescue to whatever they managed to do: sit down, go outside, drink some water. This strengthens the belief that without it the worst would have happened, and with that belief grows the fear of the next attack.

How to use it: draw the cycle using the client’s most recent attack as an example and label the links in their own words. The second link offers the most room for work: interpretations can be tested.

Caution: if the attacks began recently, a medical checkup comes first to rule out physical causes, including heart rhythm disorders or thyroid problems.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

David M. Clark’s cognitive model of panic (1986).
