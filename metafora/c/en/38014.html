Exercise

Metaphor cards: the price of my unwanted “yes”

  • Personal boundaries
  • Values
  • Right to choose
  • Self-worth

For people who agree when they want to refuse and are then angry with themselves. The cards show what a person pays for an unwanted “yes” and what it protects.

What you need: a scene deck (30 cards face up, the rest face down in a pile).

How to run

  1. 1.The client chooses the first card face up: “A situation where I agree against my own wishes” (a colleague asks them to stay after work). “What is the person on the card doing and feeling?”
  1. 2.They draw the second card blind from the face-down pile: “What do I pay for this ‘yes’?” Talk about the image first, then about the cost: an evening, sleep, anger at oneself.
  1. 3.They choose the third card face up: “What do I protect when I agree?” Often it is peace in the relationship or the image of a reliable person; don’t dismiss this.
  1. 4.The fourth card is also chosen face up: “What matters more to me than this ‘yes’?” Then: “Is what your ‘yes’ protects worth that price?”

Here is an example:

  • ▫“I pay with evenings with my daughter. I protect my reputation as someone who never lets people down. What matters to me is being home by seven.”

To close, the client picks one situation this week where they will respond differently. Practice the refusal itself with the card “Practicing ways to say no” (with teens, “The right to say ‘no’”). In a group, everyone works individually and shares in pairs.

For the facilitator: don’t interpret the cards for the client; a blind card that says nothing to the client or feels unpleasant can be replaced. If refusing could lead to threats or punishment, take care of safety first.
