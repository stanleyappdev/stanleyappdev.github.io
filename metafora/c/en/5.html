Metaphor

Passengers on the Bus

  • Anxiety
  • Self-criticism
  • Values
  • Authoring your life
  • Right to choose
  • Acting despite fear
  • Awareness of thoughts
  • Tolerating difficult emotions

KEY IDEA

thoughts are not facts

You are driving the bus of your life. At the stops, passengers get on: fears, memories, self-critical thoughts, doubts. Some of them look menacing. They come up to the driver and demand: “Turn left,” “Don’t go there, it’s embarrassing,” “Stop, you won’t make it anyway.”

The driver can argue with them and stop watching the road. They can strike a deal: “I’ll turn wherever you say, just sit quietly.” Then the passengers are silent, but the bus follows their route. Or the driver can keep hold of the wheel and head in the direction they have chosen, with all the noise in the cabin. The passengers cannot be put off the bus, but they cannot take the wheel either.

When to use it: the client gives up important steps so as not to hear the inner voices, or believes they must first get rid of them.

How to run

If the client prefers action, set out a “driver’s” chair and several “passenger” chairs. The client sits at the “wheel,” and the practitioner or group members say the passengers’ lines that the client named beforehand. The client does not argue or follow the orders, but describes where they are driving. 5-10 minutes is enough.

Questions

  • ✓Which passenger speaks the loudest? What do they usually say?
  • ✓Which routes do you avoid to keep the passengers quiet?
  • ✓Where do you want to drive your bus?
  • ✓What changes when you hear the passengers and do not hand them the wheel?

Note for the practitioner

In the role-play, use only wordings the client has named and agreed to. If the voices are linked to traumatic experience, work in imagination, without acting it out.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

The ACT metaphor “Passengers on the Bus,” described in Steven C. Hayes, Kirk D. Strosahl and Kelly G. Wilson, Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (1999). The role-play option and the questions were added editorially.
