Parable

Two artists and different paintings

  • Authenticity
  • Self-worth
  • Comparing yourself to others
  • Jealousy and envy

Two artists sat by the same window. She painted a bright city morning; he painted a quiet glint on a glass. When he saw her canvas, he began to enlarge his own: he added buildings, people, and a streetcar. There was hardly any room left for the glass.

Meanwhile, she envied his ability to notice small things. She erased the square and tried to paint only the glint. Both lost the thread of what they wanted to say.

Their teacher asked each of them: “What were you looking at before you looked at the canvas next to you?” They turned back to the window. The morning was still big, and the glint was still small. One did not cancel out the other.

The next day the artists exchanged techniques for working with light, but each chose their own subject.

For discussion: “What skill of the other person’s would I like to master? What intention of my own is important to keep while doing so? How can I tell learning apart from giving up on myself?” In this story there’s no need to decide whose painting is better.
