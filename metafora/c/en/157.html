Exercise

Working with polarities

  • Self-acceptance
  • Helplessness
  • Expressing emotions
  • Tolerating difficult emotions

Goal: explore a pair of opposite sides in oneself, be fully in each, and notice how they can coexist instead of one pushing the other out.

Approximate time: 25-40 minutes.

Preparation: free space, two places in the room (chairs or marks on the floor).

  1. 1.Find the polarity

Start from what the client brings: “I always have to be strong,” “I can’t say no.” Ask which opposite side appears less often or gets judged. Name both in simple words: “Strong” and “Helpless,” “Obedient” and “Rebellious.” If the client knows Perls’s terms, you can speak of the “topdog” (the one who demands) and the “underdog” (the one who evades).

  1. 2.Place the sides

Ask the client to decide where in the room each side stands and how far apart they are.

  1. 3.Be in the first side

The client stands in the first place and speaks in the first person: “I am Strong. I...” Suggest finding a posture, a gesture, a loudness of voice. Allow 3-5 minutes.

  • ✓What do you feel in your body in this position?
  • ✓What does this side want? What is it afraid of?
  1. 4.Be in the opposite side

The client moves to the second place and enters the opposite side just as fully: posture, voice, words. This is often where more time and support are needed.

  1. 5.Dialogue

The client moves between the places several times, and the sides address each other. Make sure neither one “wins” too quickly.

  1. 6.Close

The client returns to their usual seat.

  • ✓What new did you learn about each side?
  • ✓What can they give each other?
  • ✓In which situation this coming week will you need a bit of both?

Note for the practitioner

The aim is not to choose the “right” side but to make both available. If one side is linked to a traumatic experience of helplessness, work more gently and keep it shorter.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

The concept of polarities and the terms “topdog” and “underdog” come from Fritz Perls’s Gestalt therapy. The sequence of steps is a Metafora editorial instruction.
