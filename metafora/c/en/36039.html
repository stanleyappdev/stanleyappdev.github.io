What sadness is for

  • Sadness
  • Awareness of emotions
  • Tolerating difficult emotions

Sadness is often seen as a malfunction to be fixed as quickly as possible. In fact, it has its own work to do.

What sadness does

- It slows us down. Energy drops, we want quiet. The body takes a pause to readjust after a loss or a change.

- It helps us say goodbye to a person, a plan, a place, an image of ourselves. Without sadness, the farewell stays unfinished.

- It calls for support: tears and a sad face prompt others to come closer.

- It shows what matters: we feel sad about what we valued.

Forbidden sadness (“don’t go soft”, “others have it worse”) often does not disappear but may show up differently: as irritability, fatigue, emptiness, tension in the body.

To talk about

- What exactly are you sad about?

- How did your family treat tears?

- What do you need when you’re sad: quiet, someone’s presence, a conversation?

If low mood lasts more than two weeks, nearly every day, and joy and interest have gone, that is a reason to see a doctor or psychotherapist.
