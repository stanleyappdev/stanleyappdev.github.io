Body-based practice

Free movement

  • Expressing emotions
  • Body awareness
  • Presence

Goal: let the body move without a task and notice which impulses, sensations and images appear when there is no external aim.

Approximate time: 15-20 minutes: 5-10 minutes of movement plus conversation.

Preparation: free space without sharp corners, optionally a mat; silence is better. Agree on a signal for ending: a small bell or the words “Time to finish.”

  1. 1.Set the frame

“For five minutes, move the way your body wants to: you can stand, lie down, walk slowly, freeze. There is nothing to show and nothing has to look nice. I’ll keep an eye on the time and make sure you don’t bump into anything.” Eyes can be closed or the gaze lowered.

  1. 2.Start from stillness

“Stand or sit, notice your breathing. Wait until the first wish to move appears, and follow it.”

  1. 3.Movement

The leader stays silent and only warns if needed: “Wall on your right.” A minute before the end: “Gradually come to a finish.”

  1. 4.Come back

“Stop. Open your eyes, look around the room, feel your feet.”

  1. 5.Talk

First the client speaks on their own. The leader may describe what they saw only with consent and without interpretation: “I saw you holding your hands near your chest for a long time,” not “You were protecting yourself.”

  • ✓What was the first movement?
  • ✓Which movement did you want to repeat?
  • ✓What happened when you weren’t moving?
  • ✓Does anything from this experience echo your life right now?

In a group

Pairs: one person moves, the other sits to the side as a witness and only observes. 5 minutes of movement, a conversation in the pair under the same rules, then swap roles. The witness speaks about their own impressions and gives no advice.

With children aged 3-10

Free movement to music with “Freeze!” pauses: the child moves as they like, freezes at the pause and tells or shows who or what they just were. Eyes stay open.

Note for the practitioner

With eyes closed, people with traumatic experience may feel more anxious or unreal: offer movement with open eyes and a shorter time. If panic or numbness appears, stop the exercise and help the person return to the room: feet, objects around, talk about the present.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Close to the Authentic Movement method developed by Mary Starks Whitehouse in dance movement therapy; the role of the witness was described in detail by Janet Adler. The instructions are editorial.
