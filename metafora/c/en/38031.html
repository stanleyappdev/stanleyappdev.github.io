Exercise

The need behind my emotion

  • Awareness of emotions
  • Your own wants
  • Recognizing and naming emotions
  • Expressing emotions

An emotion is the beginning of a movement toward something. When the movement stops halfway, the feeling gets stuck. This exercise takes one emotion through the stages of the contact cycle: from the sensation in the body to the need behind it and the action it calls for. The theory of the cycle is described in the card “The contact cycle: how we meet our needs.”

How to run

  1. 1.Choose a recent episode with a distinct emotion of moderate intensity, or what you are feeling right now.
  1. 2.Sensation: where is it in the body, and what is it like?
  1. 3.Awareness: name the emotion; it can be a mix, such as hurt and anger.
  1. 4.Give it a voice: “My anger wants…”, “If my sadness could ask for something, it would ask for…”.
  1. 5.Need: “I need…” - to be heard, to rest, to protect a boundary, to be close, to be alone. Look for your own words rather than ready-made equivalents like “anger = boundary.”
  1. 6.Mobilization and action: who is this addressed to, and what realistic and safe action would meet the need? Saying something, asking, refusing, crying, going to bed earlier. Sometimes the action is directed toward yourself.
  1. 7.At which step of this episode did you linger longest or most want to stop? Does that happen in other situations too?
  1. 8.Choose one small action for the next few days.

For your notes

Emotion - Where in the body - What it wants - My need - Who it is addressed to - What action - Where I usually stop

Caution: if the emotion leads to an old painful memory, slow down: you can move on to action another time.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

The cycle of experience (sensation, awareness, mobilization, action, contact, completion and withdrawal) was described by Joseph Zinker, “Creative Process in Gestalt Therapy” (1977); it builds on the contact theory of Perls, Hefferline and Goodman.
