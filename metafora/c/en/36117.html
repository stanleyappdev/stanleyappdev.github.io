Patches on the dam

  • Children's self-esteem
  • Self-acceptance
  • Perfectionism

Mirko, a little beaver, was building a dam on his own for the first time. He hauled branches, plastered them with mud and tried very hard. But in the morning he saw water seeping through the dam. “I’m a bad builder,” said Mirko. “Everyone else’s dams are whole, and mine is full of holes.” And he hid in his lodge.

In the evening Grandpa Beaver came. “Come on, I want to show you something,” he said, and led his grandson to his own old dam. It was enormous, and there were patches all over it: light and dark, made of thin twigs and of thick boughs.

“So many patches!” Mirko said in surprise. “Did your dam leak too?”

“Hundreds of times,” Grandpa laughed. “This patch is the very first one. Here I realized the branches have to be laid crosswise. And here - that I needed more mud. Every patch is a place where I learned something.”

The next morning Mirko patched his dam. It still wasn’t as nice as Grandpa’s. But now Mirko saw it differently: each patch showed what he had learned.

Questions

  • ✓How did Mirko feel when his dam leaked?
  • ✓What “patch” do you have? What did something that once didn’t work out teach you?

After the story, the child can draw their own dam with patches and label what each one taught them.
