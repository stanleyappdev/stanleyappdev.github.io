Goodbye at the door

  • Adjustment
  • Children's fears
  • Support
  • Self-soothing

The child is five, in their first month of preschool. Every morning there are tears at the classroom door, the mother lingers, and the goodbye stretches to half an hour. Together with the mother, we built a fixed three-step goodbye ritual and rehearsed it at home for a week, playing “preschool” with toys.

The ritual:

  1. 1.The mother helps the child change and names what comes next: “You’ll play, have lunch, take a nap, and after your nap I’ll pick you up.”
  1. 2.A hug and a “secret sign”: the same little heart drawn in pen on the mother’s palm and on the child’s.
  1. 3.A short “Bye, see you this evening!” - and the mother leaves without turning back, even if the child is crying.

Every evening at home they read a short story about a little bunny who went to the forest school for the first time with Mom’s sign on its paw.

Two weeks later the goodbye had shrunk to a few minutes, and the tears passed soon after the mother left. The teacher helped: right after the third step she invited the child to a favorite activity.

What worked: predictability, the same words every day, and the fact that the mother never slipped away unnoticed. Feelings were named, not forbidden: “You’re sad that I’m leaving, and you’ll manage.”
