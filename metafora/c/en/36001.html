Inside Out 2

  • Anxiety
  • Panic attacks
  • Comparing yourself to others
  • Who am I?
  • Friendship

Riley is thirteen, and Joy has already built in her head the sense that “I’m a good person,” sending unpleasant memories far away. With adolescence, new emotions arrive at headquarters: Anxiety, Envy, Ennui and Embarrassment. At hockey camp Riley learns that her two best friends will go to a different high school. Anxiety takes over the console so that Riley gets onto the strong team at any cost, and sends Joy and the other “old” emotions out of headquarters.

The ending is revealed below. Riley tries to impress the older girls and the coach, drifts away from her friends, secretly reads the coach’s notes and gradually builds a new sense of self: “I’m not good enough.” During the deciding game Anxiety can no longer stop, and Riley has a panic attack. Joy helps Anxiety let go of the console, and Riley’s new sense of self becomes more complex: it holds both strengths and mistakes. Riley makes up with her friends.

Themes: anxiety that wants to protect against future failures; the wish to be accepted; friendship during change; a complex, imperfect view of oneself.

Who to offer it to: teens aged 11-18 with anxiety, perfectionism, or a fear of not coping on a team or at a new school. The panic attack scene is shown in detail: if the teen has panic attacks, discuss in advance whether they want to see it.

After the panic attack scene, invite the teen to describe their own “Anxiety at the console”: what it says when it takes over, and what helps it let go.

Questions

  • ✓What was Anxiety trying to protect Riley from? What is your anxiety trying to protect you from?
  • ✓What did Riley give up to impress the older girls? Is that familiar to you?
  • ✓What happens in your body and thoughts when anxiety takes your console?
  • ✓Why did the new belief “I’m not good enough” grow so fast? Which thoughts about yourself do you hear most often?
  • ✓Who or what helps you take back the controls?
