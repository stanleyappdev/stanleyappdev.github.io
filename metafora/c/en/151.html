Exercise

Imagery rescripting

  • Self-criticism
  • Personal boundaries
  • Supportive self-talk

Goal: change the emotional meaning of a painful memory by bringing into the imagined scene an adult figure who does what nobody did then: stops it, protects, takes the child to a safe place.

Approximate time: 45-60 minutes, with at least 10-15 minutes for closing.

Preparation: the client is in a stable state, a working alliance is established, a stop signal is agreed, and a stabilising exercise (for example, a safe place) has been practised.

This work requires training in schema therapy or in imagery rescripting. If the memory is traumatic or there are PTSD symptoms, appropriate training in trauma-focused therapy is necessary. Agree the depth of the work with the client’s state and readiness.

  1. 1.Agree the memory and the frame

Choose a memory linked to the current problem. Explain: the facts don’t change; what changes is how the client experiences the scene. Remind them of the stop signal.

  1. 2.Enter the scene

With eyes closed or looking down, the client describes the scene in the present tense, as the child: where they are, who is there, what is happening. Don’t go to the hardest moment in full detail: stop when the child’s need becomes clear.

  1. 3.Bring in the adult figure

The therapist or the client’s adult self enters the scene. This figure stops the perpetrator, protects the child, tells them it is not their fault and, if needed, takes them to a safe place. At first the therapist usually does this, later the client does it themselves.

  1. 4.The child’s needs

Ask the child in the image: “What do you need now?” The adult figure gives it: a hug, an explanation, play, staying close.

  1. 5.Return to the present

The client opens their eyes, feels their feet, looks around the room, says the date.

  • ✓What do you feel now?
  • ✓What changed in the scene?
  • ✓What did the child hear from the adult figure?

Agree how the client will take care of themselves after the session.

If it becomes too much

Stop the scene, bring the client back to the present through the body and gaze, use the stabilising exercise. Return to the memory only after recovery.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Imagery rescripting; developed in schema therapy by Jeffrey Young and Arnoud Arntz.
