Exercise

A day lived meaningfully

  • Meaning in life
  • Self-care
  • Values

For those who put off a meaningful life “until later”: after the renovation, when the kids are older. Frankl emphasized that meaning lies not in life in general but in a specific day and situation. So you plan not an ideal day but an ordinary one, with work and obligations, yet with room for what matters.

How to run

  1. 1.Choose a weekday in the coming week and write down what is fixed: work from nine to six, the commute, picking up your child from school.
  1. 2.Add four points of meaning, each with one action and a time:

- a deed (creative values): what I will do because it matters (“at 9:00 p.m. I write a page of my story”);

- an encounter (experiential values): whom I will truly be with, or what I will notice (“dinner without phones”);

- responsibility: what I will take on and see through (“the letter to the bank I’ve been putting off”);

- rest: how I will recharge (“a twenty-minute walk after work”).

If you run short of time or energy, scale the action down rather than crossing it out.

  1. 3.Live this day. In the evening, ask yourself: When was I truly present? What did this day ask of me, and how did I respond?

Questions for discussion

- Which point of meaning was hardest to hold on to?

- What from this day could be repeated every week?

Rest is as much a part of a meaningful day as anything else, not a reward for it.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Based on the ideas of Viktor Frankl’s logotherapy.
