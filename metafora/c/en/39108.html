Animated film

Finding Nemo

  • Anxiety
  • Control
  • Upbringing
  • Trust
  • Children's self-esteem

At the start of the film the clownfish Marlin loses his wife and almost all their eggs in a barracuda attack. Only his son Nemo survives, with a smaller “lucky” fin. Marlin raises him in constant fear. On his first day of school Nemo, angry with his father, swims up to a boat and is taken by a diver. Nemo ends up in a dentist’s aquarium in Sydney, and Marlin sets off across the ocean to find him together with the cheerful fish Dory, who can’t remember what has just happened.

The ending is revealed below. The journey forces Marlin, who is afraid of everything, to go through dangers and to trust Dory. Sea turtles show him that children can manage on their own. In the aquarium Nemo plans an escape with the other fish and finds that he can do more than he thought. When the friends finally meet, Nemo saves Dory from a fishing net, and Marlin for the first time lets his son go, telling him he can do it.

Themes: parental anxiety after loss; overprotection and trust in the child; a child’s physical differences; friendship with someone who has memory problems.

Who to offer it to: children from 5 and parents, especially anxious or overprotective ones, or those raising a child after a loss. Nemo’s mother dies at the beginning, off screen.

Questions

  • ✓Why was Marlin so afraid for Nemo? What in this was understandable, and what got in his son’s way?
  • ✓What did Nemo prove to himself in the aquarium?
  • ✓How did Dory help Marlin, even though she often forgot everything?
  • ✓For children: when do you wish adults trusted you more? What can you already do by yourself?
  • ✓For parents: what helped Marlin let his son go? What one small step of independence could you allow your child?
