Inside Out

  • Children's emotions
  • Self-regulation
  • Emotional connection
  • Awareness of emotions
  • Recognizing and naming emotions
  • Sadness
  • Adapting to change

Eleven-year-old Riley moves with her parents from Minnesota to San Francisco. In her head, at “headquarters” with a control console, live five emotions: Joy, Sadness, Anger, Fear and Disgust. Joy is used to being in charge and tries to keep Sadness away from important memories. The new city, a strange school and a cramped house are hard for Riley, and her mom asks her to stay “our happy girl.”

By accident, Joy and Sadness end up far from headquarters together with the core memories. Anger, Fear and Disgust are left at the console: Riley grows irritable, pulls away from her parents and friends, and finally decides to run away to Minnesota by bus. The ending is revealed below. On the way back, Joy realizes that one of Riley’s dearest memories began with sadness: after a lost game, her parents and friends came to comfort her. Joy hands the console to Sadness, Riley gets off the bus, comes home and for the first time tells her parents she misses her old life. Her parents admit they miss it too.

Themes: the function of every emotion, including sadness; hiding feelings for the sake of others; moving and family change.

Who to offer it to: children from 6 and their parents, especially after a move, a change of school or other family changes. It works well for watching together. The disappearance of the imaginary friend Bing Bong and the plan to run away may upset younger viewers.

After watching, invite each family member to name who is “at the console” for them at home in the evening after a hard day.

Questions

  • ✓Why didn’t Joy want Sadness to touch the memories? Do you sometimes hide your sadness too?
  • ✓What happened to Riley when only Anger, Fear and Disgust were left at the console?
  • ✓What changed when Riley told her parents she missed home?
  • ✓Which of the five emotions most often takes charge of you after a hard day? What does it say, and what helps it calm down?
  • ✓For parents: how might the request to “stay happy” have affected Riley? Are there similar requests in your family?
