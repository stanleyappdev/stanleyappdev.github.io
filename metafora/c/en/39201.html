Fairy tale

The Ugly Duckling

  • Bullying
  • Self-acceptance
  • Who am I?
  • Loneliness

In a duck’s nest under the burdock leaves, the last chick to hatch was big, gray and clumsy. In the farmyard the ducks pecked him, the hens chased him, the turkey puffed himself up and shouted at him, and even his brothers and sisters said it would be better if he had never been born. In the end the duckling ran away.

He wandered through the marshes, hid from the hunters’ dogs and stayed for a while in an old woman’s cottage, where the cat and the hen lectured him: anyone who cannot lay eggs or arch his back should keep his opinions to himself. In autumn the duckling saw beautiful white birds with long necks in the sky and felt a longing for them he could not explain. He barely survived the hard winter in the frozen reeds.

In spring his wings grew strong. The duckling swam up to the white birds, ready for them to drive him away too, and suddenly saw his reflection in the water: he was a swan himself. The swans welcomed him, and the children on the bank said the new one was the most beautiful of all. The swan did not become proud: he remembered how he had been bullied.

Questions

  • ✓What hurt the duckling most in the farmyard?
  • ✓Why did the cat and the hen think the duckling was “wrong”?
  • ✓What helped him get through the winter?
  • ✓Does it happen that a person takes a long time to find “their own people”? Where can they be found?

The story suits conversations about rejection, being different from those around you and looking for your own place. Do not reduce the ending to a promise that “one day everyone will see how beautiful you are”: how the duckling got through the hard time matters too.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Hans Christian Andersen, “The Ugly Duckling” (Den grimme ælling, 1843). Free retelling by Metafora.
