Exercise

My battery

  • Overwhelm
  • Self-regulation
  • Self-care

It’s hard for a child to say “I have no energy,” but easy to show how much charge is left in a battery. An exercise for the start of a class or group session, or for working with a child worn out by school, after-school activities, and homework. Takes 15–20 minutes.

What you need: sheets with a five-bar battery outline, colored pencils.

How to run

  1. 1.“Imagine you’re a phone. How much charge do you have right now? Color in as many bars as you have energy.” The adult colors in their own battery too.
  1. 2.To the left of the battery, children draw or write what “drained” their charge today: a test, an argument, noise at recess.
  1. 3.To the right - what recharges them: hugs, walking the dog, music, quiet.
  1. 4.In a group, anyone who wants to shows their battery and names one way to “recharge” this evening. Sharing what drains them is optional.
  1. 5.At the end: “When the charge is low, a phone switches to power-saving mode. What could yours be like?”

What to watch for

If a child shows one or two bars week after week, talk separately with them and with their parents: the load may need to be reconsidered, rather than just teaching the child to “recharge.”
