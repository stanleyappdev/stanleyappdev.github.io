Paradoxical intention

  • Anxious thoughts
  • Fear
  • Acting despite fear
  • Anxiety

A technique for anticipatory anxiety, when fear of a symptom is what brings it on: a person is afraid of blushing at a meeting - and blushes. Frankl proposed not fighting the symptom but deliberately wishing for it - in an exaggerated way, with humor.

How to run

  1. 1.With the client, describe the vicious circle: anticipating the symptom → fear → symptom → even stronger anticipation.
  1. 2.Formulate a paradoxical wish - short, exaggerated, a little funny: “Now I’ll turn as red as a beet - let the whole meeting take a look!”, “Let my hands shake so hard the coffee in my cup turns to froth,” “Tonight I refuse to sleep on principle!”
  1. 3.Practice in session: the client says the phrase out loud, and you play along. Laugh together at the symptom, but never at the client.
  1. 4.Between sessions, the client recalls the phrase as soon as anxiety appears and writes down what happened to the symptom. The wish must be sincere, if only for a moment: if the phrase becomes a trick “to make it all go away,” the effect disappears.

This is how a young physician who had feared sweating in public for four years got rid of the fear within a week, by deciding to show everyone how profusely he could sweat.

Caution: a physician should first rule out physical causes of the symptom. Do not use the technique in a depressive state or crisis, when the fear concerns real danger or traumatic memories, or with people who experience humor as invalidating.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

A technique developed by Viktor Frankl; the case of the young physician is described in Man’s Search for Meaning.
