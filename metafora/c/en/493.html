Sibling jealousy

  • Friendship
  • Comparing yourself to others
  • Negotiation
  • Children's emotions
  • Emotional connection
  • Support
  • Upbringing
  • Jealousy and envy
  • Adapting to change

After a brother or sister is born, the older child may feel both joy and jealousy at the same time. The usual routine, the parents’ availability, and the amount of attention all change. Demands like “You’re a big kid now” or “You have to love the baby” don’t help the child talk about difficult feelings.

Requests to be spoon-fed or carried more, or difficulties with skills already mastered, may appear. Not every change in behavior is explained by jealousy: it is important to notice fatigue, health, and other changes in the child’s life as well.

What can help: predictable one-on-one time with the older child; an invitation to help without being obliged to look after the baby; keeping important rituals and the right to their own things. Instead of comparisons, name the need: “You want me to spend time just with you.”

We accept feelings and stop dangerous actions: “It’s okay to be angry. I won’t let you hit the baby. I’ll help you step away and say what happened.” For discussion with parents: “What small daily ritual will show the older child that their place in the family is still there?”

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

American Academy of Pediatrics - Preparing Your Older Child for a New Baby.
