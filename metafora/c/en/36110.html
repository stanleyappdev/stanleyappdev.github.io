Game

Elephants and mice

  • Aggression in children
  • Anger
  • Self-regulation

For preschoolers, anger is easiest to express through the body. The game gives it a safe outlet and at the same time practices the ability to stop on a signal.

How to run

  1. 1.Explain: “When we get angry, we become big and loud, like elephants. And then we can become quiet, like mice.”
  1. 2.At the word “Elephants!” the children stomp in place as hard as they can, make angry faces, and may growl or trumpet. Stomping is allowed only with their feet on the floor, without touching anyone.
  1. 3.At a clap of the hands, everyone freezes like a statue.
  1. 4.At the word “Mice!” the children tiptoe around the room, squeaking softly.
  1. 5.Alternate the commands, gradually making the “elephants” shorter and the “mice” longer. End with the mice curling up to sleep and breathing slowly.

Questions

  • ✓When are you an elephant?
  • ✓What helps you turn back into a mouse?

For the facilitator: the game doesn’t teach children “not to get angry”; it shows that anger can be expressed without hurting anyone, and that you can come back to calm. If a child can’t stop, stand next to them and be a “mouse” together with them.
