Punishment vs. consequences

  • Upbringing
  • Responsibility
  • Aggression in children

Punishment involves pain or humiliation and has nothing to do with the act itself. A consequence follows logically from it: you spilled it-wipe it up; you broke someone else’s thing-replace it; you didn’t pack your schoolbag-you go without what you need.

Punishment teaches the child to avoid getting caught; a consequence teaches them to take the outcome into account.

According to Gershoff’s meta-analyses, physical punishment is consistently associated with more aggression and anxiety in children and poorer relationships with parents, and does not improve behaviour in the long run.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Draws on Elizabeth Gershoff’s meta-analyses of the effects of corporal punishment and on Rudolf Dreikurs’s concept of natural and logical consequences.
