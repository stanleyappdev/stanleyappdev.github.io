Film

Barbie

  • Who am I?
  • Authenticity
  • Roles
  • Self-acceptance
  • Perfectionism

Stereotypical Barbie (Margot Robbie) lives in Barbieland, where every day is perfect and Barbies hold every job. In the middle of a party she suddenly thinks about death, and the next morning her feet go flat. To fix things, she sets off for the real world, and Ken (Ryan Gosling) comes with her. There Barbie sees for the first time that she is seen not as a role model but as a symbol of unattainable standards: the schoolgirl Sasha bluntly explains why such dolls are disliked. It turns out that the sad thoughts “passed” to Barbie from Sasha’s mother, Gloria, who works at Mattel. Meanwhile Ken discovers patriarchy and brings it back to Barbieland.

Spoiler: Barbie is lost and decides she is good for nothing. Gloria delivers a monologue about the contradictory demands placed on women, and it is these words that help the other Barbies come to their senses. Ken admits he doesn’t know who he is without Barbie, and she advises him to find out. In the end, after talking with the doll’s creator Ruth Handler, Barbie chooses to become human, with a body, mortality and uncertainty.

Psychological thread: growing up as the loss of a perfect picture and the right to be imperfect. A separate thread follows Ken’s identity, built around another person, and another follows Gloria and Sasha, a mother and her teenage daughter. Themes: expectations and roles, perfectionism, self-image, self-acceptance.

Who to suggest it to: teens from 15 and adults who live under pressure of appearance standards or “proper” behaviour; for a conversation between a mother and a teenage daughter; people whose self-esteem rests on a single role.

Caution: the film is bright and satirical, and some viewers see it as a political statement. Before suggesting it, find out how the client feels about the film so that the conversation doesn’t turn into an argument about ideology.

Questions

  • ✓What first disrupted Barbie’s perfect world? What did the moment when your “perfect picture” cracked look like?
  • ✓Which of the demands Gloria talks about do you hear directed at you? From whom?
  • ✓Who is Ken without Barbie? Is there a role in your life without which it’s hard to say who you are?
  • ✓What do Sasha and Gloria learn about each other over the course of this story?
  • ✓Barbie chooses to be human, with all the inconvenience. Which “perfect” rules would you like to give up?

SOURCE AND ORIGIN
