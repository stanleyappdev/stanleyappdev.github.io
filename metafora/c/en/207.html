Meditation

The inner observer

  • Inner support
  • Awareness of thoughts
  • Awareness of emotions
  • Tolerating difficult emotions
  • Presence

KEY IDEA

thoughts are not facts

Goal: practice the observer stance: noticing thoughts, emotions and sensations as things that change, and oneself as the one who notices them.

Approximate time: 10-15 minutes.

  1. 1.Prepare

Sit comfortably, feet on the floor. You can close your eyes or lower your gaze. For a few breaths, simply feel the support beneath you.

  1. 2.Notice the body

“Notice one sensation in your body: warmth, pressure, tension. Name it silently: ‘I notice…’. Now notice who is noticing it.”

  1. 3.Notice an emotion

“Notice what emotion is here now. Name it: ‘I notice anxiety (tiredness, calm).’ Who is noticing this emotion?”

  1. 4.Notice a thought

“Notice a thought that has appeared. ‘I notice the thought that…’. The thought will come and go, and you notice the next one.”

  1. 5.Feel the continuity

“Sensations, emotions and thoughts have changed throughout your life. The one who noticed them in childhood is noticing them now.”

  1. 6.Close

Bring your attention back to the room: sounds, objects, support. Open your eyes, move your hands and feet.

Questions

  • ✓What did you notice?
  • ✓How did the experience change when you said “I notice…”?
  • ✓In which situations might the observer stance be useful to you?

Note for the practitioner

If during the exercise the person feels detached, or that they or the world are unreal, stop: open the eyes, name objects in the room, feel the feet. For people prone to dissociation, choose a short version with eyes open.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

The ACT “Observer Exercise”; Steven C. Hayes, Kirk D. Strosahl, Kelly G. Wilson (1999). Acceptance and Commitment Therapy: An Experiential Approach to Behavior Change, pp. 192–196.
