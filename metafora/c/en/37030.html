DEAR MAN

  • Making requests
  • Negotiation
  • Personal boundaries

Goal: prepare and rehearse a conversation in which getting what you want matters: asking for something, saying no, standing by a decision.

Approximate time: 25-40 minutes.

Materials: a sheet with the seven letters of the scheme, a pen.

D (Describe): describe the situation in facts, without judgements.

E (Express): say what you feel and think, in the first person.

A (Assert): ask or say no clearly, without hints.

R (Reinforce): explain how agreeing benefits the other person too, and thank them for it.

M (Mindful): stay focused on your goal. Repeat your point like a broken record, don’t respond to counter-accusations.

A (Appear confident): look confident: steady voice, direct gaze, no excuses.

N (Negotiate): be willing to negotiate, offer an alternative.

Example of a request (to a neighbour)

“For the last week we’ve been able to hear your music until one in the morning (D). I’m not getting enough sleep, and it’s wearing me out (E). Please keep it down after eleven (A). That way we’ll stay on good terms as neighbours (R).” To the retort “Well, you use a drill during the day”: “Maybe. Still, please keep it down after eleven” (M). “How about this: on weekdays, quiet from half past eleven” (N).

Example of a refusal (to a colleague)

“You’re asking me to cover your Saturday shift (D). I feel awkward saying no (E), but I won’t be working this Saturday (A). Better to be honest now than let you down at the last minute (R).” Under pressure: “I understand. Not this Saturday” (M). “I can cover for you next month if you tell me in advance” (N).

  1. 1.Choose the conversation

The client names a conversation that is coming up soon and states the goal in one sentence: what exactly they want to achieve.

  1. 2.Write down the lines

The client writes one or two sentences for each letter. Check that D contains only facts and that A sounds like a direct request or refusal.

  1. 3.Rehearse

The partner in the pair (in individual work, the psychologist) first agrees, then objects and pushes. The client speaks out loud without looking at the sheet and notices where they feel pulled to justify themselves. Repeat twice.

  1. 4.Discuss
  • ✓Which letter was easiest, and which was hardest?
  • ✓Where did you feel like justifying yourself or giving in?
  • ✓What helped you return to your request?
  • ✓How will you know the conversation went well, even if the other person doesn’t agree?
  1. 5.Close

Agree when the conversation will take place. Afterwards the client writes down which letters worked, and you discuss it at the next meeting.

With teenagers aged 11-14

Use situations from their lives: asking parents to let them come home later, saying no to a friend who wants to copy homework, telling a classmate not to take their things without asking. Start with three steps: what happened, what I feel, what I’m asking for. Add the other letters gradually.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

The DEAR MAN skill from the Interpersonal Effectiveness module; Marsha Linehan, DBT Skills Training Manual (2015).
