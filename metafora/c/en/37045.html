Mode map

  • Awareness of emotions
  • Who am I?
  • Recognizing and naming emotions

A worksheet for the stage when the client is already familiar with modes but doesn’t yet see how they take over from one another.

How to run

  1. 1.Ask the client to recall three or four recent situations that left a noticeable mark: an argument, a remark from a manager, an evening alone, a good day.
  1. 2.For each, write down the chain of modes in the order they appeared.

For example

  • ▫partner is late and not answering → fear of being abandoned (Vulnerable Child) → ten messages in a row (Angry Child) → a cold “I don’t care” (Detached Protector) → at night, “I ruined everything myself” (Punitive Critic).
  1. 3.Above each arrow, note what triggered the shift: a thought, something someone said, a bodily sensation.
  1. 4.Give the modes names in the client’s own words: “Little One,” “Concrete Wall,” “Prosecutor.”
  1. 5.Mark where the Healthy Adult or the Happy Child appeared, even for a moment.

Questions

  • ✓Which shifts keep repeating?
  • ✓Which mode switches on the fastest, and which one lasts the longest?
  • ✓Where could the chain still have been stopped?

The map is added to from session to session and becomes a shared language for the psychologist and the client.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Based on Jeffrey Young’s schema therapy mode model.
