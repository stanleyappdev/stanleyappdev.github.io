Visualization

Symboldrama: Bird

  • Right to choose
  • Closeness
  • Awareness of emotions

An imagined bird opens up themes of freedom and limits, closeness and distance.

Approximate time: 30-40 minutes, of which 15-20 minutes in the imagery. Running it requires training in symboldrama (katathym imaginative psychotherapy).

How to run

  1. 1.A comfortable position, eyes closed or gaze lowered, a minute or two of calm breathing.
  1. 2.“Imagine a bird. Look at where it is and what it is doing.”
  1. 3.Ask open questions: what kind of bird it is, where it is, what it is doing. A cage or an injured wing should come from the image itself, not from your prompting.
  1. 4.The client can come closer and, if they wish, briefly become the bird, look at the world through its eyes, and then become themselves again.

Questions

  • ✓What does the bird do as you come closer?
  • ✓If it is in a cage or tied up, what is holding it?
  • ✓What does it want right now?

What to watch for

- Freedom or confinement (a cage, a tied leg, an injured wing), distance, the client’s feelings toward the bird.

- Don’t rush to let the bird out of the cage: ask what it needs and let the client decide.

How to close

Say goodbye to the bird and notice whether it stays or flies away. Feel your feet, take a deeper breath, stretch and open your eyes. What was most vivid? Suggest drawing the bird at home.

Caution: an injured or dead bird can stir up grief - take your time and don’t interpret anything. The client can stop at any moment. If anxiety is strong, help them return to a safe place within the image, or ask them to open their eyes and feel their feet. Do not use in an acute crisis or with people who have difficulty telling the imagined from the real.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

An additional motif in symboldrama (Hanscarl Leuner’s katathym imaginative psychotherapy).
