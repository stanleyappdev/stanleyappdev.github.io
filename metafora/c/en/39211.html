Fairy tale

The Mitten

  • Friendship
  • Personal boundaries
  • Adjustment

An old man was walking through the forest with his little dog running behind him. He dropped a mitten and did not notice. A mouse ran up, climbed into the mitten and decided to live there. A frog hopped up:

- Who lives in the mitten?

- Scritchy-Scratchy Mouse. And who are you?

- Croaky Frog. Let me in too!

- Come in.

Then came Hoppity Hare, after him Sister Fox and Brother Wolf, and each time the residents squeezed up a little and let one more in. When Tusky Boar arrived, there was hardly any room left, but they let him in too. Last of all came the bear. The animals said it was already crowded, but the bear begged so hard that they let him in as well. The mitten stretched so much it was about to burst.

Meanwhile the old man noticed that his mitten was gone and went back to look for it. The little dog ran ahead, saw the mitten moving and began to bark. The animals took fright and scattered in all directions, and the old man picked up his mitten and went home.

Questions

  • ✓How did the animals decide whom to let in?
  • ✓How did those already living in the mitten feel when a new guest arrived?
  • ✓What happened when it got really crowded?
  • ✓When do you feel like letting someone join the game, and when do you feel like saying, “There’s no more room”?

The story suits younger children and groups: hospitality, welcoming newcomers, and the fact that space and strength have limits. It is easy to act out with toys or in roles.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Ukrainian folk tale. Free retelling by Metafora.
