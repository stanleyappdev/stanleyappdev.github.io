Exercise

Metaphor cards: what remains important

  • Loss
  • Support
  • Awareness of emotions
  • Tolerating difficult emotions
  • Sadness

A quiet exercise for those grieving a loss: a person, a home, a period of life, a relationship that has ended. It doesn’t rush the person to “let go” but helps them keep a connection with the good that was there. About 20 minutes.

What you need: an imagery deck, 30 cards face up; tissues.

How to run

  1. 1.Agree with the client on whom or what they want to remember today.
  1. 2.“Choose a card that reminds you of something good that once was.” Ask: “What is on it? What memory does it bring back?”
  1. 3.Then talk about the memory itself: “Where are you in it? Who is nearby? What can you hear, what does it smell like? What was most precious about it?”
  1. 4.The client chooses the second card face up as well: “How do I want to keep this memory?” They might carry on a tradition, write down a favorite phrase, tell their children, or simply remember.

Example

  • ▫a card with flowers on a windowsill - “Mom always kept geraniums on the windowsill. I’ll plant some at my place and tell my daughter how her grandma looked after them.”

To close, come back to the present: “What do you need right now to get back to your day?” The cards can be photographed.

For the facilitator: don’t interpret the cards for the client.

Caution: tears are welcome here; don’t rush the client or hurry to comfort them. In the first weeks after the death of a loved one, limit the work to steps 1–2 and simply listen. Work just as carefully with someone who has recently lost their home or still has no stable housing: first attend to support in the here and now, and return to memories when the person is ready.
