Exercise

Check the facts

  • Anxious thoughts
  • Awareness of thoughts
  • Anger
  • Fear

KEY IDEA

thoughts are not facts

An exercise for moments when an emotion seems too big for the situation, or when a person doubts whether they “have the right” to feel this way. The goal is not to calm yourself down but to find out whether the emotion and its intensity fit the facts.

How to run

  1. 1.Name the emotion and rate its intensity from 0 to 10.
  1. 2.Describe the event using facts only: “My manager wrote, ‘Come see me at four,’ and nothing else.”
  1. 3.Write down your interpretation (“I’m going to be fired”) and at least three other explanations: they want to discuss the project, they were in a hurry, they always write briefly.
  1. 4.Is there a threat? What exactly is it, how likely is it, and how will you cope if the worst happens?
  1. 5.Conclusion: does the emotion fit the facts fully, partly or not at all? And its intensity?

An emotion fits the facts if:

fear: there is a real threat to your health, safety or something important to you;

anger: you or people close to you are being blocked from reaching something important or are being wronged;

sadness: you have lost something valuable;

guilt: you have acted against your own values;

shame: people who matter to you really might reject you if they found out about an action or a trait.

If the emotion fits the facts, look for ways to change the situation; if it doesn’t, or it is too intense, opposite action will help.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

The Check the Facts skill from the Emotion Regulation module; Marsha Linehan, DBT Skills Training Manual (2015).
