Parable

The fence and the nails

  • Anger
  • Conflict
  • Personal boundaries
  • Responsibility
  • Communication
  • Resentment
  • Aggression in children

There was once a hot-tempered young man: the smallest thing made him lose control, and he said hurtful words to people. One day his father gave him a bag of nails and asked him to hammer one nail into a fence post every time he failed to hold back his anger.

On the first day the young man hammered in several dozen nails. Over time there were fewer and fewer: hammering nails turned out to be harder than stopping and waiting for the outburst to pass.

At last came a day when he did not lose his temper even once. His father said that now, for each such day, he could pull out one nail. Weeks went by, and the post was empty.

Then the father led his son to the fence:

- You did well. But look at these holes. The post will never be the same. When we hurt someone in anger, a mark remains, even if we apologize later.

Questions

  • ✓What changed in the young man while he was hammering in and pulling out the nails?
  • ✓What helps you notice anger before it turns into words?
  • ✓What “holes” are left in your relationships, and what can be done about them now?
  • ✓Do you agree that the mark stays forever? What can help a relationship recover?

Note for the practitioner

The story works well for a conversation about responsibility for one’s words. Make sure it does not turn into an accusation: a person who is already ashamed of their outbursts benefits more from discussing how to repair relationships after a quarrel than from hearing that the harm is irreversible.
