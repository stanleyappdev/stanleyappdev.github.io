Body-based practice

A point of calm

  • Self-soothing
  • Body awareness
  • Resources
  • Safety

A first step before more demanding body work: find a place in the body that feels pleasant or at least neutral, and learn to return to it when things get hard.

How to run

  1. 1.Invite the client to sit comfortably: feet on the floor, eyes open.
  1. 2.Say: “Slowly move your attention through your body and find a place where everything is okay.”

For example

  • ▫warm palms on the thighs
  • ▫the back resting against the chair, the feet, the earlobes.

Neutral works too: many people find it easier to locate “nothing special” than something pleasant.

  1. 3.Ask the client to describe the sensation in detail: temperature, weight, density, size, edges.
  1. 4.Let the client simply stay with this sensation for 30–60 seconds. What happens with the breathing and the rest of the body?
  1. 5.Give the point a short name (“warm palms”); if it helps, the client can touch this place with their own palm.
  1. 6.Practice: briefly notice an area with mild tension, then return to the point. Repeat 2–3 times.

At home: three times a day, for a minute, find the point in calm moments so that it is already at hand in a difficult moment. If no such place can be found in the body, start from the outside: a warm cup in the hands, a smooth pebble.

Caution: for people with traumatic experiences, some areas (chest, belly, throat) may trigger anxiety or unpleasant memories, so start from the periphery - the palms, the feet. If numbness or fear appears, stop and ask the client to look around the room.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Draws on the work with bodily resources in Peter Levine’s Somatic Experiencing; see his book In an Unspoken Voice (2010).
