Exercise

Metaphor cards: what is in my hands

  • Circle of influence
  • Control
  • Decision-making
  • Helplessness
  • Anxiety

A child’s exams, a parent’s illness, changes at work - and the person feels helpless or tries to control everything. The exercise helps separate what the person can influence from what doesn’t depend on them, and find a first step. About 20 minutes.

What you need: an imagery deck (30 cards face up, the rest face down in a pile); a sheet with three nested circles: in the center, “I do it myself”; around it, “I influence it together with others”; on the outside, “Doesn’t depend on me”; sticky notes.

How to run

  1. 1.The client chooses the first card face up: “Which card reminds me of the situation that worries me?” Ask: “What is happening here? Where are you in this picture?”
  1. 2.The client writes each part of the situation on a separate sticky note and places it in the matching circle.
  1. 3.The client chooses the second card face up and places it in the center: “What is my first step?” Turn the image into a single action.
  1. 4.The client draws the third card blind and places it on the outer circle: “How can I relate to what doesn’t depend on me?” The client first describes the image, then says what it suggests.

Here is an example:

  • ▫a son has exams.

In the center - “make a study schedule with him, keep an eye on his sleep”; in the middle circle - “talk to his teacher”; in the outer one - “what questions will be on the exams.”

To close, the client writes down the first step with a date and one sentence for the outer circle: “I do what I can, and the rest is not in my hands.”

For the facilitator: don’t interpret the cards for the client; a blind card that says nothing to the client can be replaced.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

The idea of the circle of influence: Stephen R. Covey, “The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People” (1989). The work with metaphorical cards and the steps of the exercise are editorial instructions by Metafora.
