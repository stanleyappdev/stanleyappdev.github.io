Body-based practice

TIPP

  • Self-soothing
  • Panic attacks
  • Body awareness
  • Tolerating difficult emotions

When arousal is so intense that thinking is impossible, these techniques change the body’s state within a few minutes. The effect is short-lived, but it buys time for other skills.

T (Temperature): lean over a bowl of cold but not icy water (no colder than 10 °C / 50 °F), hold your breath and immerse your face for 30 seconds so that the water covers your cheeks and the area under your eyes. Instead of a bowl, you can hold a bag of cold water to your eyes and cheeks.

I (Intense exercise): a few minutes of running in place, squats or climbing stairs.

P (Paced breathing): slowed breathing for 1–2 minutes - inhale for a count of 4, exhale for 6–8.

P (Paired muscle relaxation): muscle relaxation paired with the breath - on the inhale, tense your muscles (fists, shoulders, abdomen), not to the point of cramping; on the exhale, say “letting go” silently and relax.

How to teach

Try each technique in session while the client is calm, rating tension (0–10) before and after. Warn a person with panic attacks that a racing heart and breathlessness after exercise may resemble an attack.

Caution: cold water sharply slows the heart rate, and exercise speeds it up. If there is heart disease, a slow pulse, low blood pressure, eating disorders, pregnancy, an allergy to cold or medications that affect the heart rate, consult a doctor first. With injuries or asthma, choose moderate movement. Hold your breath only as long as it is comfortable; if you feel dizzy or unwell, stop.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

The TIP skills (often written TIPP) from the distress tolerance module; Marsha Linehan, DBT Skills Training Manual (2015).
