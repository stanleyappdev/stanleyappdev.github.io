Exercise

Significant witnesses

  • Closeness
  • Support
  • Inner support
  • Self-worth

Change grows stronger when someone sees it. This conversation looks for people - real or imagined, present or from the past - who have already noticed in the client what the client is now developing in themselves.

How to run

  1. 1.Choose a quality or change the client is currently strengthening: “I’m learning to say ‘no,’” “I’ve become calmer with my kids.”
  1. 2.“Who in your life would be least surprised to learn this about you? Who has already noticed this in you at some point?” This could be
  • ▫a grandmother
  • ▫a teacher
  • ▫an old friend
  • ▫someone
  • ▫who has passed away
  • ▫even the author of a favorite book.
  1. 3.Look through this person’s eyes: “What did they see in you? Which of your actions could have shown them this? What would they say if they saw you now?”
  1. 4.About mutual contribution: “What did this person give you? What did you give them? What might it have meant to them to know you?”
  1. 5.The client chooses a step: tell a real witness about the change, write a short letter in the witness’s voice, or decide whose voice should carry more weight in their own life and whose less.

In a group (outsider witnesses): one person spends about 10 minutes telling the story of their change. Then 2–3 listeners, who have agreed in advance not to give advice or praise, respond: which words struck them; what picture of the teller’s values formed for them; what in their own lives it resonates with; what they are taking away with them. Finally, the teller says what was important to them in what they heard.

Caution: if the witness has died, grief may come up - make room for it. Do not choose people who have caused the client pain.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Michael White’s re-membering conversations (the “club of life”) and outsider witness practice, described in his book Maps of Narrative Practice (2007).
