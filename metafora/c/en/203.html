Exercise

Supported by the chair

  • Panic attacks
  • Safety
  • Self-soothing
  • Body awareness
  • Presence

Goal: help the client feel the body’s support and calm down by consciously handing their weight over to the chair and the floor.

Approximate time: 1-3 minutes; can be done right in the middle of a conversation.

Preparation: a chair with a backrest.

  1. 1.Find the points of support

“Notice all the places where your body touches the chair and the floor: feet, buttocks, thighs, back, maybe elbows on the armrests.”

  1. 2.Hand over the weight

“Let the chair hold you a little more. Imagine that with each out-breath the weight goes into these points of support. You don’t have to hold anything up yourself.”

  1. 3.Notice the change
  • ✓Where is the support felt best?
  • ✓What has changed in your shoulders, your breathing, your hands?
  1. 4.Close

Tell the client they can come back to this exercise at any point in the session or at home when tension rises. Others won’t notice it.

With a child aged 7-10

“Imagine you are a bag of sand slowly pouring into the chair.” You can ask the child first to tense up like a wooden soldier and then become “floppy like a rag doll.”

Note for the practitioner

If the person feels sleepy or “disconnected” after the exercise, ask them to sit up straighter, open their eyes and look around the room.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

A widely used short grounding practice based on the body’s support. The author has not been identified; the instructions are editorial.
