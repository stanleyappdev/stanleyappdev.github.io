Art technique

Tear up the anxiety

  • Anger
  • Anxiety
  • Expressing emotions

Goal: express outwardly the tension of anxious thoughts and then decide what to do when such thoughts return.

Approximate time: 15-20 minutes.

Materials: a few sheets of thin paper (old newspapers, printer paper), a pen, a box or bag for the pieces.

  1. 1.Write the thoughts down

“Write down the anxious thoughts that are going round in your head right now. Each one separately or all together. A single word is fine.” The client decides whether to read what they wrote aloud.

  1. 2.Tear it up

“Now tear the sheet with your hands any way you like: hard, fast, into small pieces.” Several sheets can be torn. The practitioner doesn’t hurry the client.

  1. 3.Notice your state
  • ✓What did you feel while tearing?
  • ✓What changed in your body: hands, breathing, shoulders?
  • ✓Did any thought come up while you were tearing?
  1. 4.Decide what to do with the pieces

The client chooses: throw them away, collect them in a box and close it, or glue some onto a new sheet and write a supportive phrase next to them. Ask why they chose this.

  1. 5.Close

Anxious thoughts may come back. Discuss what the client will do then: write the thought down and set it aside for a “worry time,” take a few slow breaths out, reach out to someone. Choose one option.

With a child aged 7-10

The child can draw the worry instead of writing. The adult supports: “Tear it the way you want.” Question: “What is your worry like now: bigger, smaller or the same?”

Note for the practitioner

Tearing releases tension but does not remove the cause of the anxiety. Don’t promise that the thoughts will go away. If the anxiety is linked to a real problem, return to it after the exercise.
