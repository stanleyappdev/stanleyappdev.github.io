Belly breathing for children

  • Anxiety
  • Self-soothing
  • Body awareness
  • Children's emotions

Goal: help the child notice the movement of the belly during calm breathing and have a simple way to calm down while sitting, for example in class.

Approximate time: 5-10 minutes.

Preparation: a chair; clothing that does not press on the belly.

  1. 1.Explain

“When we’re worried, our breathing often gets fast and seems to move up into the chest. Let’s try breathing so that the tummy moves.”

  1. 2.Place the hands

The child puts one hand on the belly over the clothes and the other on the chest. “Breathe as usual. Which hand moves more?”

  1. 3.Breathe with the belly

“As you breathe in, your tummy puffs up a little under your hand; as you breathe out, it slowly goes down.” Count together: in for three, out for four. 5-8 calm cycles, with no holds and no very deep breaths.

  1. 4.Discuss
  • ✓How did your hand move?
  • ✓What changed in your body after a few breaths?
  • ✓Where could this breathing be useful to you: before a test, after an argument, before sleep?
  1. 5.Close

Return to ordinary breathing. Together think up an unnoticeable version for class: a hand on the belly under the desk and three slow out-breaths.

With children aged 3-6

Use the game with a toy on the belly (the card “Belly breathing”) or suggest “blowing up the tummy like a balloon”.

With teenagers aged 11-14

Invite the teen to choose a comfortable count themselves and discuss in which situations this breathing can be done without others noticing.

Note for the practitioner

If dizziness, tingling or discomfort appear, stop and breathe normally.

SOURCE AND ORIGIN

Comfortable-breathing principle from NHS, “Breathing exercises for stress.”
