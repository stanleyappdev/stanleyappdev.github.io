Exercise

Metaphor cards: a triptych of myself

  • Who am I?
  • Authenticity
  • Self-presentation
  • Your own wants

Three cards in a row, like the panels of a triptych: how others see me, who I am, who I want to be. For someone who feels they are not seen as they are, or who doesn’t know who they want to become.

What you need: a deck with people, animals and objects (30–40 cards face up, the rest in a face-down pile).

How to run

  1. 1.The client chooses three cards face up and lays them in a row: on the left, “How others see me” (clarify who exactly); in the center, “Who I am”; on the right, “Who I want to be.” In individual work, adults and older teens may draw the left card face down, blind: other people’s view is only partly known.
  1. 2.About each one: “What do you see? What does this image say about you?”
  1. 3.“What do others see that isn’t in the middle card? What from the right card is already in the middle one?”
  1. 4.“Which of the people close to you would you show the middle card to? What from the right card could you try this week?”

Here is an example:

  • ▫on the left, a sturdy oak - “reliable”
  • ▫in the center, a bird on a branch - “tired”
  • ▫on the right, a bird flying with its flock - “lighter, among my own.”

To finish, the triptych is photographed. In a cohesive group, participants, in pairs assigned by the facilitator, give each other a “How I see you” card with kind words; anyone may decline it. If bullying comes up in the conversation about the left card, talk with the participant separately.

For the facilitator: don’t interpret the cards for the client; a blind card that says nothing can be replaced. You can speak to younger teens more informally.
