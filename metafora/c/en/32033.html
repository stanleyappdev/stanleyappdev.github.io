Game

The director and the living tableau

  • Team building
  • Communication
  • Nonverbal communication
  • Roles
  • Friendship

Goal: practice agreeing on a shared action with words: asking clearly, accepting a refusal and offering another option.

Approximate time: 20-30 minutes.

Participants: 6-15; a director, actors, spectators.

Materials: none; free space.

  1. 1.Explain the rules and roles

The director creates a “living picture”: they make up a scene, for example “At the fair”, “In a hospital for toys” or “Space station”, and with words offer each person a role and a place. An actor may agree, suggest a different pose or role, or stay a spectator. The director does not move anyone with their hands and does not insist.

  1. 2.Build the picture

Allow 3-4 minutes for building. The director addresses each person by name: “Please stand by the door and be an apple seller.” The spectators silently watch which requests work.

  1. 3.Freeze

When the picture is ready, everyone freezes for 5-10 seconds. The director explains what is shown, and the actors briefly say how they felt in their roles.

  1. 4.Change the director

Everyone who wants to takes a turn as director; it is not compulsory.

Discussion

  • ✓Which requests were easy to carry out? Why?
  • ✓How did the director react when someone chose a different role?
  • ✓What helped you agree without arguing?

Closing

Everyone names one good request they heard today. Everyone “unfreezes” together and stretches.

With teenagers

Scenes can be more complex: “Break time at school”, “Film set”, “Startup meeting”. The director can ask the actors to suggest how they see their role themselves.

If someone mocks

Roles must not humiliate or ridicule anyone (for example, “you’ll be the trash”). If this happens, stop the game, offer the participant another role of their choice and remind everyone of the rule. Do not draw conclusions about children’s character from the role they chose.
